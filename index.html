Lec 01 Antennas & Signal Propagation

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Antenna: A transducer that converts electrical signals to EM waves (Tx) and vice versa (Rx).
  • Isotropic Radiator: Theoretical point source radiating equal power in all directions (3D sphere).
  • Friis Transmission Equation: Relates transmitted power, antenna gains, distance, and wavelength to received power.
  • Path Loss: Reduction in signal power density as it spreads over distance.

📐 Key Formulas

Friis Transmission Equation Pr = Pt Gt Gr (λ / 4πr)2
Pr: Received Power
Pt: Transmitted Power
Gt, Gr: Antenna Gains (linear, not dB)
λ: Wavelength (m)
r: Distance (m)
Use for: Calculating received power in free space Line-of-Sight (LOS).
Free-Space Path Loss (FSPL) in dB Lp(dB) = 20 log10(r) + 20 log10(f) - 147.56
r: Distance in meters
f: Frequency in Hz
Note: If using km and GHz, constant becomes +92.45.

⚡ Quick Reference

Antenna Type Pattern Usage
Wire (Dipole/Monopole) Omnidirectional (Donut) Radio, Cellular (Towers)
Aperture (Horn/Dish) Directional Satellite, Radar, Microwave
Microstrip (Patch) Directional/Flat Wi-Fi, Mobile phones, GPS

📝 Common Exam Questions

Calculate the received power given Pt, antenna gains, frequency, and distance. Use Friis equation. Calculate λ = c/f first. Ensure gains are linear, not dB.
Explain the difference between Link Budget and Friis Equation. Friis is ideal/theoretical (free space only). Link Budget includes real-world losses (cables, atmosphere, misc).

Lec 02 Antenna Radiation Patterns & Power

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Radiation Pattern: Graphical representation of radiation properties (strength vs. direction).
  • HPBW (Half-Power Beamwidth): Angle between points where power is half (-3 dB) the peak.
  • Directivity (D) vs. Gain (G): D focuses energy; G includes efficiency (η). G = η × D.
  • EIRP: Effective Isotropic Radiated Power (Power × Gain relative to isotropic).

📐 Key Formulas

Antenna Gain (Aperture) G = (4π Aeff) / λ2
Aeff: Effective aperture area (m²)
λ: Wavelength
Note: Larger dish/antenna = Higher Gain.
EIRP (Logarithmic Scale) EIRP (dBm) = Pt (dBm) + Gt (dBi) - Line Loss (dB)
Used to determine the actual strength of the signal leaving the antenna.
EIRP to ERP Conversion ERP (dB) = EIRP (dB) - 2.15 dB
Because a Dipole has 2.15 dBi gain relative to an Isotropic source.

⚡ Quick Reference

  • dBi vs dBd: dBi is gain relative to isotropic (0 dBi). dBd is gain relative to dipole (0 dBd = 2.15 dBi).
  • Lobes: Main Lobe (desired direction), Side Lobes (unwanted, waste energy), Back Lobe (opposite direction).
  • Efficiency: η = ηc (conductor) × ηd (dielectric) × ηm (mismatch).

📝 Common Exam Questions

Convert Transmitter Power (mW) and Gain (dBi) to EIRP (dBm). Convert mW to dBm (10 log10(mW)). Then simply add the gain and subtract line losses.
Compare EIRP and ERP. ERP is compared to a dipole antenna. EIRP is compared to an isotropic source. EIRP is always 2.15 dB higher than ERP for the same antenna.

Lec 03 Propagation Models

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Propagation Models: Mathematical predictions of signal strength (Empirical vs. Theoretical).
  • LOS vs. NLOS: Line-of-Sight (clear path) vs. Non-Line-of-Sight (blocked, uses reflection/diffraction).
  • Multipath Propagation: Signals arrive via multiple paths causing interference.

⚡ Quick Reference: Wave Phenomena

Phenomenon Definition Typical Cause
Reflection Bouncing off large surfaces Buildings, Earth, Water
Diffraction (الانحناء) Bending around corners/obstacles Hills, Roofs (Huygens' principle)
Scattering (التشتت) Reflection off rough/small objects Foliage, Street signs, Rain (5G)
Refraction Bending due to density change Ionosphere (enables long-range radio)

⚡ Quick Reference: Models

  • Okumura: Based on Tokyo measurements. Graphical. Basis for others.
  • Hata Model: Empirical formula. Valid 150-1500 MHz. Urban/Suburban/Open.
  • COST-231: Extension of Hata for higher frequencies (up to 2 GHz). Used for GSM/PCS/LTE.

📝 Common Exam Questions

Identify the propagation phenomenon described (e.g., signal reaches behind a hill). If it bends around an obstacle, it's Diffraction. If it bounces off, it's Reflection.
Compare Empirical vs. Theoretical models.

Lec 04 Fading in Wireless Environments

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Fading: Fluctuation in signal strength at the receiver.
  • Large-Scale Fading: Path loss (distance) + Shadowing (obstacles).
  • Small-Scale Fading: Rapid changes due to multipath (constructive/destructive interference).
  • Doppler Shift: Frequency shift due to relative motion.

📐 Key Formulas

Doppler Shift fd = (v / c) × fc
fd: Doppler frequency shift (Hz)
v: Velocity of receiver (m/s)
c: Speed of light (3×108 m/s)
fc: Carrier frequency (Hz)
Antenna Spacing (Diversity) d = λ / 2
Rule of thumb: Space antennas at least half a wavelength apart to ensure uncorrelated fading paths.

⚡ Quick Reference

Fading Type Cause Characteristic
Flat Fading Channel BW > Signal BW Affects all frequencies equally
Frequency Selective Signal BW > Channel BW Distorts signal (ISI)
Fast Fading High mobility (Doppler) Rapid fluctuations
Slow Fading Shadowing/Large obstacles Gradual average power change

📝 Common Exam Questions

Calculate the Doppler shift for a car moving at 30 m/s using a 2 GHz frequency. Use fd = (v/c) * f. Ensure units match (m/s).
How does Spatial Diversity work?

Lec 05 Fade Margin & Fresnel Zones

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Fade Margin: Extra "cushion" of power (dB) added to the link budget to handle unexpected fading/interference.
  • Fresnel Zone: Ellipsoid area around the Line-of-Sight path where radio waves spread. Obstruction here causes signal loss.

📐 Key Formulas

Fade Margin FM = RSSactual - Rxthreshold
Calculated by subtracting the minimum required signal (Receiver Sensitivity) from the actual received signal strength.
1st Fresnel Zone Radius (at midpoint) r = 8.66 × √(D / f)
D: Total distance (km)
f: Frequency (GHz)
Rule of thumb: Keep at least 60% of the 1st Fresnel zone clear (0.6 × r).

⚡ Quick Reference

  • Typical Fade Margins:
    • Urban Cellular: 10-20 dB
    • Microwave Links: 20-40 dB (High!)
    • Wi-Fi Indoor: 10-15 dB
  • Improving Margin: Increase Tx power, Use High-Gain Antennas, Diversity, Lower Frequency.

📝 Common Exam Questions

A link needs 20 dB margin. The receiver sensitivity is -80 dBm. What RSS is needed? RSS = Threshold + Margin = -80 + 20 = -60 dBm.
Calculate the radius of the Fresnel zone at the midpoint for a 10km link at 5 GHz. Use r = 8.66 * sqrt(10/5). Then calculate 60% clearance.

Lec 06 Microwave Link Design

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Link Budget: Accounting for all gains and losses to ensure received power > sensitivity.
  • Earth Curvature: The earth bulges, requiring higher antennas for long links.

📐 Key Formulas

Link Budget Equation (Solve for Pr) Pr = Pt + Gt + Gr - Lp - Lsys - Lmisc
All values in dB. Gains are (+), Losses are (-).
Earth Curvature Height (h) h ≈ D2 / (8 × R)
D: Distance (km)
R: Earth Radius (~6371 km)
Used to calculate minimum antenna height to overcome the bulge.

⚡ Quick Reference: Design Steps

  1. Calculate FSPL (Free Space Path Loss).
  2. Calculate Antenna Gains (from diameter/efficiency).
  3. Set Receiver Sensitivity.
  4. Solve Link Budget for Required Pt (or Margin).
  5. Calculate Fresnel Zone Clearance.
  6. Add Earth Curvature + Fresnel Clearance to get Antenna Height.

📝 Common Exam Questions

Calculate the minimum transmit power needed for a 50km link at 11GHz with 1m dishes. Calculate FSPL -> Calculate Gains -> Use Link Budget formula: Pt = Pr - Gt - Gr + Lp + Losses.
Why is antenna height critical in long-range microwave?

Lec 07 Satellite Fundamentals

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Satellite: Object orbiting a larger body. Artificial satellites are used for Comm, Nav, Observation.
  • Orbits: Defined by Altitude and Period.

⚡ Quick Reference: Orbit Comparison

Type Altitude Period Key Use
LEO 160 – 2,000 km 90 – 120 min Starlink, ISS, Earth Observation (Low latency)
MEO 2,000 – 35,786 km 2 – 12 hours GPS, Galileo, Navigation
GEO 35,786 km 24 hours (Fixed) TV Broadcasting, Weather (High latency ~240ms)
HEO Elliptical 12 – 24 hours Coverage of high latitudes (Russia/Arctic)

📝 Common Exam Questions

Which orbit is used for GPS and why? Compare GEO and LEO.

Lec 08 Satellite Segments & Transmission

🔑 Core Concepts

  • Segments: Space (Satellite), Ground (Stations/Control), User (Terminals).
  • Uplink: Earth → Satellite (Higher Freq).
  • Downlink: Satellite → Earth (Lower Freq).
  • Transponder: Device on satellite that Receives → Amplifies → Frequency Shifts → Retransmits.

⚡ Quick Reference: Frequency Bands

Band Uplink (GHz) Downlink (GHz) Application
L 1.6 1.5 GPS, Mobile (Satellite phones)
C 6 4 TV, Fixed Services (Less rain fade)
Ku 14 11-12 DTH TV, VSAT (Smaller dishes)
Ka 30 20 High-Speed Internet (Starlink, High Bandwidth)

Lec 09 Satellite Navigation Systems

🔑 Core Concepts

  • GNSS: Global Navigation Satellite System (e.g., GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou).
  • Trilateration (التثلث): Determining position by intersecting spheres from multiple satellites.
  • Requirement: At least 4 satellites (3 for position X,Y,Z; 1 for time correction).

⚡ Quick Reference: GNSS Comparison

System Country Orbit Civil Accuracy
GPS USA MEO ~5 m
GLONASS Russia MEO ~5-10 m
Galileo EU MEO <1 m (High Accuracy)
BeiDou China MEO + GEO <1 m (Regional)

🔑 Core Concepts (DGPS)

  • Differential GPS (DGPS): Uses a fixed ground station at a known location to calculate errors and send corrections to nearby receivers.
  • Benefit: Improves accuracy from meters to centimeters.
